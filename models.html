How they were built

Agent speed, analyst standards.

The workflow behind the workbooks, why it adds value, and what separates these from the models you usually see.

The workflow

AI-built, human-standard

Claude agents with MCP integrations handled SEC EDGAR research and data extraction, Python and openpyxl generated the workbooks, and every change ran through automated recalculation and balance tie-out checks before shipping. Nothing goes in unchecked.

The value

Weeks of work in days, defensibly

Research, build and audit cycles compress from weeks to days without dropping the standard: every input cited to a source, every assumptions refresh logged, every scenario re-tied. Speed you can still defend in front of an investment committee.

The difference

Not your usual spreadsheet

Most models are static files: hardcoded inputs, no audit trail, stale the day after they're built. These carry a live market-data layer, bull/base/bear scenario engines, automatic variance flags and a browser dashboard — closer to a working product than a workbook.

Inside the models

What's under the hood.

The four things to know before you open them: how they're built, how they're audited, where the market data comes from, and how each company is benchmarked.

Architecture

Fully linked three-statement mechanics

12 tabs per workbook. Revenue is built bottoms-up: compute economics for the AI name, cohort math (CAC, churn, NRR) for the SaaS name, origination take rates for the bank. It then flows through regional scenario engines into income statement, balance sheet and cash flow. Balance sheets tie in every period, under every scenario.

Variance & audit

Guidance-based budgets, logged changes

FY2025 budgets are seeded from each company's actual published guidance and flagged automatically when variance crosses a 10% threshold. A Sources tab cites every input with a link and last-updated date; a Version History tab logs every assumptions refresh: what changed, when, and why.

Market data

A live market layer with refresh stamps

A Python pipeline pulls the Treasury yield curve and SOFR from US Treasury and NY Fed data feeds and equity quotes from the Yahoo Finance API, restamping every file on refresh. An implied-valuation cross-check then prices each model's own forecast against live EV/Revenue and P/E multiples.

Competitor analysis

Two comps, industry medians, takeaways

Each company is benchmarked against two public comps plus industry medians: growth, margin and Rule-of-40 buckets for the software names, and P/E, ROE and price-to-tangible-book norms for the bank. Every line carries a written "what it means" read, with takeaways a hiring committee can argue with.
